# AudioMirror

## Register

product

## Product purpose

Duplicates one audio output to as many devices as the user wants, with the
lowest latency the platform allows, correct formats picked automatically, and
devices that reconnect on their own after being unplugged.

## Users

People who want one sound in two places at once: a film in headphones and on
the living-room speakers, a rehearsal in monitors and in an interface, a stream
on the built-in output and on a capture device. They are comfortable with
system settings but are not audio engineers. They open the panel to wire things
up, then never again until something breaks.

## Physical scene

Someone at a desk or on a sofa, at any hour, in whatever light. The application
lives in the tray and is opened for fifteen seconds at a time. It has no
opinion about the time of day, so it wears whatever the operating system wears:
light and dark are equal citizens, neither is the default.

## Tone

Plain, exact, unhurried. State what is happening and what it costs. Never
enthusiastic, never apologetic. Numbers are facts, not achievements.

## Anti-references

- Neon gradients and glowing LED meters. Nothing here is a nightclub rack.
- Rounded coloured badges, pill counters, decorative status dots.
- Emoji, arrows as decoration, em dashes.
- SaaS chrome: hero metrics, feature cards, marketing tone inside a tool.
- Skeuomorphic hi-fi pastiche: brushed metal, knobs, VU needles.
- Terminal brutalism: full-monospace layouts, ASCII rules, fake command lines.

## Strategic principles

1. **The tool disappears into the task.** A system panel, not an application
   with personality. What moves is what is alive: the meters. Everything else
   holds still.
2. **Say the real number.** Measured latency, not the requested setting.
   Underruns, not a green tick. A user who cannot see the cost cannot make a
   decision.
3. **Absence is a state, not an error.** An unplugged device stays listed and
   keeps its settings. The interface says it is waiting, and waits.
4. **No dialogue box for anything.** Adding a destination, changing a setting:
   it happens in place, in the panel.
